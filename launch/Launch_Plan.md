# Ashyq Dala: 21 days to test paid demand

Prepared 6 October 2026. This is an execution plan and a pricing experiment, not an income forecast. The first commercial objective is one paid job that you can deliver well. A live website alone will not produce revenue.

## The offer to sell

**One Kazakhstan company. A concise English research brief. USD 250 for the first scoped pilot.**

The customer is an equipment exporter or supplier evaluating an unfamiliar Kazakh buyer, distributor or intermediary in an active deal. The second route is a research/advisory firm buying a bounded research task under its own review. Ask for a real task with a decision date. An old Kazakhstan installation makes a company worth approaching; it does not make it a qualified buyer.

Deliver a 2–4 page brief plus a source log: company identity and name matching, available records, comparison with documents the client provides, material discrepancies, unanswered questions, and dated links. Distinguish source statements from your inference. Include one clarification round within the original scope. Agree the fields and available sources before quoting a two-business-day delivery window. Never promise inaccessible records.

Test USD 250, with USD 125 collected after scope acceptance and USD 125 due on delivery under the agreed terms. Deposits finance delivery; preserve enough cash to finish or meet the agreed cancellation/refund terms. Confirm the customer can use your invoice and payment method before starting. For agencies, a paid mini-assignment can be agreed separately; do not accept a long unpaid trial. The USD 250 price has not been validated by customers.

## Today: make the offer usable

1. Add your actual business contact, truthful identity and a payment method you can receive. Test a request from a different device or inbox. Agree how confidential client documents will be exchanged.
2. Read the sample and remove any claim you cannot personally support. A fictional demonstration must be conspicuously labeled. A real report needs primary source links and human review.
3. Identify 10 people who already know your work: factory suppliers, equipment contacts, family business introductions, or relevant classmates/alumni. Ask for an introduction to someone with a current Kazakhstan transaction. Existing trust is the first priority.
4. Review the nine cold prospects in the workbench. They were selected from public company sources, not from expressed purchasing interest. Pick four that match your actual knowledge. Add a specific sentence from the factual reason in the list.
5. Block 90 minutes for outreach and follow-up, 45 minutes for conversations, and the remaining work time for paid delivery. Keep website refinements below 30 minutes a day unless a buyer reports a blocker.

## The 21-day schedule

| Days | Work | Evidence to record |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Set up a usable contact and invoice route; prepare one sample; request 10 warm introductions; approach four selected prospects yourself. | A working intake test, list of reachable buyers, messages actually sent. |
| 3–5 | Approach about five relevant new prospects per working day; follow up when appropriate; hold short qualification calls; quote only deliverable scopes. | Decision being made, deadline, missing information, person approving USD 250, next action. |
| 6–7 | Follow up once with earlier prospects, respond to objections, and seek the first agreed scope and deposit. If a scope is accepted, prioritize delivery. | Deposit received, or exact reason a qualified prospect declined. |
| 8–10 | Deliver any first pilot; review every factual claim; invoice the balance; ask whether the brief helped and what was missing. Ask permission to use any testimonial. | Delivery time, direct cost, balance collected, correction count, useful customer feedback. |
| 11–14 | Continue the segment producing actual conversations. Ask satisfied clients for one relevant introduction. Send agencies a specific paid-capacity offer. | Qualified conversations and paid jobs per segment; elapsed time from first reply to payment. |
| 15–18 | Repeat the smallest useful scope. After two paid jobs delivered well, test USD 350 on the next comparable inquiry; explain what is included and record the result. | Acceptance/rejection at each price, hours spent, contribution per job. |
| 19–21 | Deliver commitments, collect balances, review the experiment. Continue only the channel with credible paid demand and workable delivery economics. | Cash actually received, earned revenue, remaining obligations, costs, time, repeat/referral demand. |

These activity counts are planning targets, not promised conversion rates. Work with the time you actually have. Limit simultaneous pilots to your delivery capacity, initially two.

## Short messages to adapt and send yourself

Warm introduction:

> I'm starting a focused research service for suppliers evaluating a Kazakhstan buyer or distributor. One company, a short English brief, dated sources and questions still needing answers. Do you know someone with a live deal where the paperwork or company information is hard to reconcile? I'd appreciate an introduction.

Exporter outreach:

> Hi [name], I saw [specific Kazakhstan project/export reference]. I prepare short English briefs on a Kazakh buyer or distributor: available company records, inconsistencies in supplied documents, and questions your team still needs answered. I'm testing a USD 250 pilot for one company, with scope agreed first. Is there an active deal where that would save your export team time? I can send the sample format.

Research agency outreach:

> Hi [name], I saw your work on [specific regional service]. I'm available for a small paid Kazakhstan desk-research assignment: source reconciliation, a dated evidence log and an English draft for your team's review. Would overflow support on a bounded task be useful this month? I can share a sample and confirm the scope before committing.

One follow-up, roughly four business days later:

> Following up on the Kazakhstan research note. If this is relevant, I can scope one company around the question your team needs answered. If there isn't an active need, I'll leave it here.

Do not claim language fluency, professional credentials, inside contacts, corporate clients or access you do not have. Use relevant public business contact routes; stop when asked. These drafts have not been sent.

## The 12-minute qualification call

- What decision are you making, about which legal entity, and by what date?
- What information is missing, contradictory, or taking your team too long to obtain?
- What have you already checked? What documents can you lawfully share?
- Who will use the brief, and who approves the payment?
- Would the agreed USD 250 scope answer the immediate question? If not, what specific result would?

Then send a short written scope: entity/BIN if known, client question, included checks, exclusions, sources/inputs, deadline, price, deposit, delivery and cancellation terms. Decline work you cannot perform. Additional entities, major translation, interviews, deep litigation research or ongoing monitoring require separate scope and pricing.

## Cash, revenue and profit: hypothetical arithmetic

Assume four USD 250 jobs are sold and completed, all invoices are collected, direct data/payment costs total USD 25 per job, other business expenses total USD 60, and each job takes five delivery hours. These are invented planning inputs, not measured costs or demand.

| Measure | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| Cash received from four deposits before delivery | 4 × USD 125 | USD 500 |
| Remaining delivery obligations | Four jobs still to complete at that moment | Not disposable profit |
| Final collected cash after all balances | 4 × USD 250 | USD 1,000 |
| Earned service revenue after completion | 4 × USD 250 | USD 1,000 |
| Direct job costs | 4 × USD 25 | USD 100 |
| Contribution before other expenses | USD 1,000 − USD 100 | USD 900 |
| Illustrative profit before tax and owner pay | USD 900 − USD 60 | USD 840 |
| Time value check | USD 840 ÷ (20 delivery hours + 20 sales/admin hours) | USD 21/hour |

Taxes, refunds, unpaid invoices, currency conversion and actual overhead may change the result. Receiving USD 500 in deposits is different from earning USD 500 in profit. Record the three figures separately. Under the same invented cost assumptions, six fully paid jobs would leave USD 1,290 before tax and owner pay: (6 × USD 250) − (6 × USD 25) − USD 60. Neither scenario predicts you will find those customers.

## Stop rules that protect the next three weeks

- **No website expansion until paid demand.** Fix broken intake or misleading copy; postpone databases, automated scraping and subscription features.
- **After ten qualified buyer conversations and zero paid acceptance**, stop selling the same scope unchanged. Ask which gap mattered: no urgency, an adequate existing tool, insufficient trust, wrong deliverable, or price. Offer one smaller paid task only if those conversations support it.
- **After around 30 relevant approaches with few replies**, inspect targeting, sender/contact accuracy and the opening message. Silence is weak evidence about the service itself. Do not buy ads to resolve that uncertainty.
- **By day 7 with no near-term paid opportunity**, allocate at least half your work time to a service people already trust you to deliver: supplier coordination, document organization, research assistance or translation only where you are competent. Ask existing contacts for a specific paid task this week.
- **By day 14 with no accepted scope/deposit and no buyer with a concrete budget and decision date**, stop treating this as the answer to the immediate cash need. Pursue paid work through existing relationships while leaving the offer available. This is a deadline decision, not proof the business could never work.
- **After a paid pilot**, use the real hours and direct costs to adjust scope or price. A job that repeatedly takes ten hours at USD 250 needs revision before you scale it.

## Source and delivery discipline

Use public primary records where accessible and information the client may share. Check the terms of any paid source before including its material in a client deliverable: access to a database does not itself grant redistribution rights. Record access date and retrieval limits. Obtain additional permission where required. Do not imply an automated sanctions certification, credit opinion, legal conclusion or guarantee of safety. Potential matches and allegations need careful disambiguation and attribution. Keep unnecessary personal data out of the brief. A lack of findings can reflect missing coverage; it is not proof of low risk.
